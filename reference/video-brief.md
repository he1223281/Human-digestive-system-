# Video brief: Nutrition in Human Beings (Grade 7)

## Decisions already made
- Tool: Remotion (React), 1920x1080, 30 fps, MP4 output.
- Level: Class 7, NCERT *Curiosity* Chapter 9 "Life Processes in Animals" (see `chapter-9-life-processes-in-animals.pdf`). Keep every fact consistent with this chapter.
- Length: 4–6 minutes.
- Voiceover: none. Teacher narration appears as large on-screen captions.
- Style: shaded, semi-realistic illustrated anatomy drawn in SVG (true 3D rendering is not possible in Remotion); natural human body proportions, no robotic mannequin.

## Full prompt from the user

Create a high-quality, scientifically accurate educational video on "Nutrition in Human Beings" for Grade 7 students.
The video should feel like a professionally produced educational science animation, not a collection of slides with basic transitions. It must explain the concepts visually, step-by-step, so that a Grade 7 student can understand what happens to food inside the human body, where it happens, how it happens, and why each organ is important.

### 1. Target audience
- Grade 7, age about 11–13, Science, topic Nutrition in Human Beings / Human Digestive System, NCERT/CBSE Grade 7 level.
- Simple, clear English, like a good science teacher explaining visually in class.
- Avoid unnecessarily advanced terminology; explain every scientific term simply when introduced.
- The objective is to make students understand the complete journey of food through the human body, not merely to show the digestive system.

### 2. Core requirement: human anatomy must look human
- Realistic but educational anatomical model with natural proportions: head, neck, shoulders, chest, abdomen, arms, hands, pelvis, legs, with natural curves and contours.
- Do NOT use a robotic mannequin, straight rectangular torso, humanoid robot, stiff geometric body parts, or organs floating inside a transparent robot.
- Semi-realistic anatomical visualization: clean, recognizable, proportional, age-appropriate, medically sensible, visually appealing.
- Show the digestive system with a transparent/cutaway view or a controlled transition from the external body to the internal organs.

### 3. Scientific accuracy (more important than visual effects)
- Verify every biological statement and animation. Do not invent organs, processes, wrong organ positions, wrong food pathways, wrong enzyme functions, wrong movements or wrong absorption mechanisms.
- Food pathway: Mouth → Pharynx → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus.
- Clearly distinguish the digestive tract from accessory organs (liver, pancreas, salivary glands). Never show food passing through the liver or pancreas.

### 4. Video structure (a coherent lesson)
- **Part 1, What is Nutrition?** A Grade 7 student eating a normal meal. Ask "What happens to the food after we eat it?" Define nutrition = the process by which organisms obtain and use food for energy, growth, repair and maintenance. Why we need food: energy, growth, repair of damaged tissues, proper functioning of the body. Simple visual examples.
- **Part 2, Why can't we use food directly?** A plate of food. Complex food substances must be broken down into simpler substances the body can absorb and use. Define digestion: "Digestion is the process of breaking down complex food into simpler substances that can be absorbed and used by the body." Use a scientifically appropriate analogy (breaking a large object into smaller usable pieces).
- **Part 3, The human digestive system.** Show the normal external body first, then transition smoothly into an anatomical cutaway. Label organs one at a time (Mouth, Oesophagus, Stomach, Small intestine, Large intestine, Rectum, Anus, Liver, Pancreas, Salivary glands), never all at once, highlighting one organ at a time.
- **Part 4, Mouth and buccal cavity.** Zoom into the head: teeth, tongue, salivary glands, saliva. Food enters the mouth. Mechanical digestion: teeth break food into smaller pieces (food is actually chewed). Chemical digestion: saliva mixes with food; saliva contains salivary amylase, which begins digestion of starch. The tongue mixes food with saliva; the bolus forms and moves to the back of the mouth. Explain "bolus" simply.
- **Part 5, Swallowing and oesophagus.** Side/cutaway view of swallowing; the bolus enters the oesophagus. Demonstrate peristalsis. Do NOT animate food falling by gravity; show coordinated muscular contractions of the oesophageal wall behind the food pushing it down, slowly enough to follow. "Peristalsis is the wave-like movement of muscles that pushes food through the digestive tract."
- **Part 6, Stomach.** Correct anatomical position, not a simple floating bag. Muscular wall, food entering from the oesophagus, churning, gastric juices mixing with food. The stomach stores food temporarily, churns and mixes it, adds digestive juices, and begins important digestion, especially of proteins. Food becomes a semi-liquid mixture. Grade 7 terminology.
- **Part 7, Liver and pancreas.** Liver in its correct location; it produces bile; bile travels to the small intestine and helps digest fats by emulsifying them into smaller droplets. State explicitly: "Bile is not a digestive enzyme. It helps in the digestion of fats by breaking large fat droplets into smaller droplets." Then the pancreas: pancreatic juice enters the small intestine and contains enzymes that digest carbohydrates, proteins and fats.
- **Part 8, Small intestine** (one of the most detailed sections). Long, coiled small intestine; digestion is completed here; partially digested food enters; digestive juices act on it. Make DIGESTION ≠ ABSORPTION very clear: "Digestion breaks complex food into simpler substances." "Absorption is the movement of these digested nutrients from the small intestine into the blood."
- **Part 9, Villi.** Intestinal wall, zooming progressively into villi, then a single villus: finger-like, thin wall, blood capillaries. Villi greatly increase the surface area for absorption. Show nutrients visibly crossing into the capillaries: Food → digestion → nutrients → absorption → blood → body cells. Nutrients must never simply disappear.
- **Part 10, Large intestine.** Undigested material moves into the large intestine, which mainly absorbs water and some salts; the contents become more solid; movement toward the rectum and anus.
- **Part 11, Egestion.** Egestion = removal of undigested food from the body through the anus. Distinguish absorption (digested nutrients → blood) from egestion (undigested material → outside the body). Do not confuse egestion with excretion.

### 12. Complete food journey
A visually impressive summary: one small piece of food travels continuously through Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus, with a brief caption at each stage (MOUTH: chewing + saliva; OESOPHAGUS: peristalsis; STOMACH: churning + gastric juices; SMALL INTESTINE: digestion + absorption; LARGE INTESTINE: water absorption; RECTUM: temporary storage; ANUS: egestion). This is the culmination of the lesson.

### 13. Animation quality
- Every animation must obey real biological movement. Avoid robotic movement, randomly rotating organs, organs changing shape or position incorrectly between scenes, food teleporting or passing through wrong organs, arrows pointing to the wrong structures, labels moving unpredictably, impossible body proportions.
- Smooth camera moves, zooms, anatomical reveals, transitions, highlighting, particle/nutrient movement, muscle contractions. Slow processes down enough for Grade 7 students to observe.

### 14. Visual style
Polished educational science-documentary style for school students: semi-realistic anatomy, natural proportions, clean textures, realistic but not disturbing organs, soft studio lighting, clear labels, clean backgrounds, high contrast between organs and background, smooth camera, professional typography, minimal clutter. Organs recognizable without being graphic.

### 15. Diagrams must be excellent
Dedicated explanatory diagrams for important concepts: peristalsis (oesophagus cross-section: bolus → muscular contraction → downward movement), stomach digestion (food + gastric juice + churning), villi (small intestine → villus → capillaries → nutrient absorption), and a clean labelled diagram of the entire tract. Accurate, symmetrical where appropriate, clearly labelled, no overlapping labels, no arrows pointing to wrong structures.

### 16. Text and typography
For a 65-inch classroom TV: large readable fonts, no tiny labels, high contrast, short important text, nothing important near the screen edges, no overcrowding, consistent typography, labels on screen long enough to read.

### 17. Narration
Natural teacher-like narration: clear, energetic, curious, friendly, accurate; not robotic. Use questions to keep students engaged, e.g. "What do you think happens to the food after we swallow it?" / "Does food simply fall into the stomach?" / "No! Our oesophagus uses a special wave-like muscular movement called peristalsis." Ask occasional prediction questions before revealing answers. (Delivered as on-screen captions, since there is no voiceover.)

### 18. Interactive "Think About It" moments
Short question, brief pause, then reveal. Examples:
- "Why do we need to chew food?" → "Because chewing breaks food into smaller pieces and increases its surface area for digestive juices to act on."
- "Does digestion happen only in the stomach?" → "No. Digestion begins in the mouth and continues through the digestive tract."
- "Where does most absorption of nutrients take place?" → "Small intestine."

### 19. Error-checking system
Before finalizing, check every scene for:
- Anatomy: organ positions, proportions, connections, food pathway.
- Biology: processes, enzyme functions, definitions, and the distinction between digestion, absorption, assimilation and egestion.
- Animation: no impossible movements, teleporting, wrong arrows, unexplained floating organs, or food in the wrong structure.
- Typography: no spelling mistakes, overlapping or unreadable labels, or labels pointing to the wrong organs.
- Continuity: the same anatomy model throughout.

If a process can't be animated accurately, use a clean explanatory diagram instead. Scientific accuracy always takes priority over visual complexity.
